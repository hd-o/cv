# Engineering a Competitive Technical CV

The OpenAI, Google, and AWS positions all demand senior engineers with strong AI/ML and software-engineering expertise, a track record of building real systems, and excellent communication skills.  For example, OpenAI’s **Applied AI Engineer (Startups)** role requires “5+ years of experience as a software engineer…ideally in a startup environment” (founder/founding experience is *“highly valued”*) and fluency in Spanish, English, and Portuguese.  It also calls for proficiency in Python and JavaScript and a history of building AI prototypes and end-to-end solutions.  Similarly, Google’s **Forward Deployed Engineer, Gen AI** calls for 5+ years developing “production-grade AI-driven solutions” on cloud platforms (e.g. GCP), including data pipelines with vector databases and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) architectures.  It explicitly seeks candidates who can lead technical discovery and solve integration challenges between AI models and customer systems.  

The AWS roles emphasize embedding deeply with customers to deliver scalable AI products.  The **AWS Senior Forward Deployed Engineer** writes “production-grade software and own outcomes end-to-end” for enterprise AI deployments, including architecting multi-agent workflows, retrieval systems, and reliability frameworks.  It requires mentoring other engineers and driving adoption of AI at scale.  The **AWS Sr AI Engineer, AI Engineering (LATAM)** (a Solutions-Architect–style role) similarly demands designing “end-to-end technical solutions” aligned to customer business needs, with deep AWS/cloud expertise and 10+ years of experience.  Key qualifications across these roles include strong software/cloud foundations (e.g. APIs, distributed systems, security), hands-on experience with LLMs or multi-agent AI, and the ability to communicate technical strategies to both engineers and business leaders.

In short, **candidates must demonstrate**: extensive software development and AI experience (preferably with Python/JS and cloud platforms), a proven ability to deploy real systems (especially in enterprise or startup settings), and strong outcomes (cost savings, performance gains, or new products launched).  They must also show the interpersonal skills to guide customers – for instance, OpenAI expects an ability to “build novel applications on our API” and deliver high-fidelity feedback to product teams, while AWS seeks those who can communicate across disciplines and lead project teams.  Language skills (Portuguese/Spanish) are explicitly required for the São Paulo-based roles.

## General CV Best Practices

Across all these technical roles, recruiters favor **clear, concise resumes that focus on impact** rather than duties.  Industry guidance stresses writing bullet points as self-contained statements of achievement, including context and metrics.  For example, Dice’s career-advice column urges candidates to *“add one or two lines to bullet points that address not just the ‘what’ but the ‘why’ and ‘how’ behind your achievements,”* such as:  

- *Before:* “Utilized Python to develop automated testing frameworks.”  
- *After:* **“Designed and implemented an automated regression test suite in Python that reduced QA testing time from five days to one day, cutting release-cycle time from two weeks to three days.”**.  

This kind of context – describing the challenge, actions, and concrete result – signals impact.  Recruiters note that “specific numbers stand out” when scanning resumes, so quantifying results (e.g. “increased system throughput by 40%” or “scaled API to 50,000 daily users”) is highly effective.  Where possible, tie each bullet to business value or scale (customers served, revenue growth, performance improvement).  For these roles, examples might include “led deployment of a GenAI chatbot serving 1M+ users” or “architected cloud migration saving 30% in operational costs,” etc.  

Formatting should support quick scanning: use **3–5 bullet points per job**, bold job titles/companies, and left-align dates.  Avoid dense paragraphs or generic sections (recruiters ignore objective statements and hobby lists).  Instead, you can open with a short professional summary that frames your profile (e.g. “Backend Engineer with 8+ years building scalable AI platforms”), but don’t waste space on “Seeking a challenging role” clichés.  If included, a summary should **position you for the role** (highlighting relevant domain, level, and successes) rather than listing unrelated history.  Finally, tailor the resume’s **language and keywords** to match the job: use terms directly from the posting (e.g. “generative AI,” “agentic workflows,” “LLM,” “cloud architecture,” “solutions engineering,” etc.) and include related skills (e.g. “prompt engineering,” “vector databases,” “multi-agent frameworks”).  A context-optimized resume “weaves skills into the narrative” rather than in isolation, improving both ATS matches and human comprehension.  

> **Key Takeaway:** Focus each bullet on *what you achieved* and *how* – quantify impact whenever possible. Use active, domain-specific verbs (e.g. “spearheaded,” “architected,” “scaled,” “deployed”) and provide context (team size, budget, technology stack) so reviewers immediately see relevance.  

## Role-Specific CV Emphases

While all positions value the above general principles, each role has its own emphasis:

- **OpenAI Applied AI Engineer (Startups):** Emphasize *startup* experience and cross-cutting projects. Highlight any roles as a founder or at early-stage companies where you wore multiple hats (engineering, product, bizdev). Cite projects where you took prototype code from proof-of-concept to production. Show fluency in required languages (Portuguese/Spanish/English) and any generative-AI work (e.g. fine-tuned models, ChatGPT-based tools). Describe how you collaborated with customers or internal teams – for example, “partnered with an early-stage fintech to build an NLP proof-of-concept,” “integrated OpenAI API into an existing product.”  Stress outcomes for customers (e.g. “improved model accuracy by 25%”) and mention open-source or community contributions if relevant.  

- **OpenAI Applied AI Architect (Brazil):** Focus on *enterprise/architecture* credentials. Highlight experience in solutions architecture, tech account leadership, or consulting. For instance, note large migrations, multi-system integrations, or regulatory compliance projects (“architected HIPAA-compliant AI service on AWS”). Detail collaborations with senior stakeholders, translating ambiguous requirements into architectures. Mention design of distributed systems, data pipelines, security or identity features (OpenAI expects “software and cloud architecture foundations, including APIs, distributed systems, data integration, identity, security”). Emphasize any leadership of complex multi-team projects and written materials (whitepapers, runbooks) supporting customer adoption. Quantify outcomes (e.g. “drove 40% cost reduction in data processing for a major bank”).  

- **Google FDE (Gen AI, Portuguese/English):** Highlight *AI production deployment* and cloud experience. List specific GenAI projects: e.g. “built a recommendation engine using PaLM/Gemini on Vertex AI,” or “implemented a RAG pipeline with BigQuery and a vector DB.” Stress your fluency in Portuguese (and English) for local client communication. Show familiarity with Google Cloud tools (Vertex AI, BigQuery, Apigee, etc.) and advanced techniques (LangChain, multi-agent frameworks). Include examples of leading discovery or discovery workshops (“led a series of design sprints with a Fortune-500 team”) and iterate on ambiguous problems. Demonstrate “developer” skills too: mention your contribution of prototypes or code, not just high-level planning. Use metrics: “reduced inference latency by X%,” or “improved model accuracy by Y points,” which matches Google’s focus on measurable outcomes.  

- **AWS Forward Deployed Engineer:** Emphasize *AWS/cloud and generative AI expertise*. List AWS services you have built with (EC2, SageMaker, Lambda, etc.) and any certifications. Describe “agentic AI” work or use of AWS AI services (Bedrock, Lex, etc.) in production. Highlight architectural work for scale and reliability: e.g. “designed multi-AZ Kubernetes architecture handling 10k req/sec.” Show leadership and mentoring (“led a team of 4 engineers to deploy a PoC in 2 weeks”). Since AWS FDEs **embed with customers**, note any consulting or customer-facing roles. Give examples of establishing best practices (CI/CD pipelines, observability frameworks), as AWS values “defining operational standards”. If you were a tech lead or senior engineer, mention how you mentored others (AWS asks for mentoring/lead experience). Quantify project scope: number of users, systems integrated, or performance gains.  

- **AWS Sr AI Engineer, AI Engineering LATAM (Solutions Architect role):** Stress solving business problems with AI. Cite large-scale projects and customer impacts. For example: “architected AI-driven supply-chain optimization that saved 15% in logistics costs.” Show deep AWS skill: “pioneered a serverless analytics pipeline using S3, Athena, and Python Glue scripts”. Include any involvement with sales cycles or sales engineering (“supported pre-sales RFPs with technical architecture proposals”). Demonstrate communication skills: AWS expects you to serve as a trusted advisor, so mention presentations or workshops you delivered. Lean on AWS’s Leadership Principles implicitly by highlighting **customer obsession** (going above-and-beyond for clients) and **ownership** (e.g. you left projects with operational docs and trained teams). Use metrics of business value (cost reduction, time saved, revenue generated) since the role description emphasizes “drive real business outcomes”.  

## Formatting and Presentation

- **Layout:** Use a clean, professional layout. One or two pages is fine for senior roles; ensure the most relevant roles and achievements appear first. According to recruiting surveys, recruiters spend only a few seconds on an initial scan, so front-load each bullet and section. List jobs in reverse-chronological order, clearly marking titles, companies, dates, and locations. Left-align dates (right-aligned dates can force distracting eye movement).  

- **Bullet Points:** Limit to ~3–5 bullets per role. Start each bullet with a strong verb (“Designed,” “Led,” “Scaled,” etc.) and avoid first-person pronouns. Highlight one achievement or responsibility per bullet and, as much as possible, quantify it (numbers, percentages, dollar amounts). If a bullet risks becoming a multi-sentence explanation, consider splitting or tightening it.  

- **Skills and Keywords:** Instead of a stand-alone “Tech Skills” list, integrate technical keywords into your experience descriptions. For instance, rather than just listing “Python, AWS, Kubernetes” in isolation, embed them naturally: e.g. “Led development of a Kubernetes-based microservice in Python on AWS, improving deployment speed by X%.”  This aligns with modern ATS and human readers (Dice notes that listing skills alone is often insufficient; it’s better to show how you used them). Ensure you use the exact keywords from the job description (e.g. “Generative AI,” “OpenAI API,” “multi-agent systems,” “cloud architecture”) since semantic screening tools match on those terms.

- **Avoid Pitfalls:** Do not hide gaps with “creative formatting” or use graphics, logos, or nonstandard fonts, as ATS parsers may skip them. Skip a “References available upon request” line or unrelated personal interests. Keep the resume focused on your most relevant accomplishments. Typos or inconsistencies are immediate red flags. For senior candidates, two pages are acceptable; but if you have fewer than ~10 years’ experience, keep it to one page and focus strictly on technical achievements.  

## Conclusion

A compelling CV for these AI-focused engineering roles must **demonstrate both technical depth and real-world impact**. Tailor each section to show how your background matches the role’s needs (e.g. startup vs. enterprise experience, cloud tools, languages). Emphasize results and lessons learned from each major project. Recruiters and hiring managers will look for evidence that “you have done a similar job and achieved measurable results” – in other words, that you can step in and contribute immediately. By using clear bullets, quantifying outcomes, and aligning your terminology to the job description, you make it easy for reviewers to see your fit. Finally, remember that OpenAI in particular “is not credential-driven”; unique or unconventional experiences (like leading a hackathon project or founding a startup) can strengthen your story if they’re clearly relevant. In sum, focus on impact, clarity, and direct relevance to the AI engineering and deployment focus of each role – those qualities will make your CV stand out to technical recruiters and hiring managers alike.
